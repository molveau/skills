# Source quotes: Emil Kowalski and those he cites

Keep these in mind; they encode the philosophy behind the rules.

## On taste & abundance
- "In a world of scarcity, we treasure tools. In a world of abundance, we treasure
  taste." (Anu Atluru, quoted by Emil Kowalski)
- "Almost every 'taste' decision has a logical reason if you look close enough." (Emil Kowalski)
- "Everyone's software is good enough these days." (Emil Kowalski)
- "Beauty is generally underutilized in software so you can use it as leverage to
  stand out." (Emil Kowalski)
- "Putting into words why something feels right trains your ability to articulate your
  judgment, a skill that will be incredibly valuable in the AI era." (Emil Kowalski)
- "It comes down to trying to expose yourself to the best things that humans have done
  and then try to bring those things into what you're doing." (Steve Jobs, quoted by Emil)
- "His course is about building great interfaces, not great demos." (shadcn (on Emil's work))

## On invisible detail
- "All those unseen details combine to produce something that's just stunning, like a
  thousand barely audible voices all singing in tune." (Paul Graham, quoted by Emil)
- "The goal is to make the user interface feel natural by incorporating those seemingly
  invisible details. They are 'invisible' because they align with users' inherent
  expectations." (Emil Kowalski)
- "Small details like this add up and make the experience feel more polished. Even if
  they go unnoticed." (Emil Kowalski)

## On animation
- "Easing is the most important part of any animation. It can make a bad animation feel
  great." (Emil Kowalski)
- "Nothing in the real world changes instantly." (Emil Kowalski)
- "The best animation is no animation." (Emil Kowalski)
- "Great animations feel natural." (Emil Kowalski)
